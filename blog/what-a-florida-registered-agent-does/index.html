What a Florida Registered Agent Does and Why You Need One

Short answer: a registered agent is the person or business that receives legal process, official notices and demands on behalf of a company, and forwards them to the company. Florida law requires every limited liability company to designate and continuously maintain a registered agent and a registered office in Florida. Without one, an LLC cannot be formed, and an existing LLC can lose important rights.

This guide is general information, not legal advice. Questions about a specific lawsuit, notice or compliance matter should be directed to a licensed attorney.

What does Florida law require?

Section 605.0113 of the Florida Statutes requires each Florida LLC, and each foreign LLC with a certificate of authority to do business in Florida, to designate and continuously maintain in the state:

  • A registered office, which may be the same as the company’s place of business in Florida.
  • A registered agent, whose business address must be identical to the address of the registered office.

The requirement is continuous. It applies from the day the company is formed for as long as the company remains active.

Who can serve as a registered agent?

Florida permits three types of registered agent:

  1. An individual who resides in Florida and whose business address is the registered office.
  2. A domestic business entity that is an authorized entity, such as a corporation or LLC, whose business address is the registered office.
  3. A foreign entity authorized to transact business in Florida that is an authorized entity, with the same address requirement.

The agent must also file a written statement accepting the appointment. The statement confirms that the agent is familiar with and accepts the obligations of the position. When a company is formed, this acceptance is included in the Articles of Organization.

What does a registered agent actually do?

The statute defines the core duty plainly. The registered agent must forward to the company, at the address the company most recently supplied, any process, notice or demand that is served on or received by the agent. In practice, this includes:

  • Service of process, such as a summons and complaint in a lawsuit.
  • Official notices from state agencies.
  • Correspondence that requires timely attention.

A reliable agent receives these documents during business hours, records them, and forwards them promptly so the owner has the full response period available. Diler Services forwards received mail the same day.

If an agent resigns, Florida law also requires the agent to notify the company at its most recent address.

Why does the registered agent matter so much?

The registered agent is the official point of contact on the public record. Three practical consequences follow.

Missed notices carry real risk

If legal papers are served and not forwarded, a company may not learn of a lawsuit in time to respond. The consequences of a missed deadline can be significant. A dependable agent reduces that risk.

Compliance affects access to the courts

Florida law provides that an LLC that has not complied with the registered agent requirement may not prosecute or maintain an action in a Florida court until it complies and pays the amounts required, which can include a court-ordered penalty.

The address becomes public

Information filed with the Division of Corporations is part of the public record. An owner who serves as the agent places that address on the public record. Many owners prefer to use a commercial agent’s address instead.

Can the owner serve as the registered agent?

An owner who resides in Florida and has a qualifying address in the state may serve as the company’s agent. This arrangement requires the owner to be available at that address during normal business hours. For owners who live in another state, travel frequently, or work from a home address they prefer to keep private, a commercial registered agent is the practical choice. Out-of-state owners forming a Florida company will find the full process in How to Form a Florida LLC From Any State.

How does a company change its registered agent?

A company can change its registered agent by filing the appropriate statement with the Division of Corporations, and the change can also be made on the annual report. The Florida Department of State lists a $25 fee for a change of registered agent. Diler Services can prepare the change as part of an amendment or during the annual report filing.

What should owners look for in a registered agent?

Owners should evaluate a provider on a few clear points:

  • Reliability: a staffed Florida address that is available during business hours.
  • Speed: a defined forwarding practice. Diler Services forwards mail the same day it is received.
  • Clear pricing: a flat fee with no surprise renewals.
  • Compliance support: reminders for the annual report and help with related filings.
